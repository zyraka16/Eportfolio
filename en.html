Role models

Career paths that inspire me

Three engineer profiles, and the international mobility opportunity each of them inspires in me.

Role model 1 · Space

Thomas Pesquet

A Supaéro engineering graduate (Toulouse) specialising in satellites, he then worked as an engineer at CNES on the autonomy of space missions, before becoming an ESA astronaut.

What I take away: an engineering career that started in Toulouse, and the link between automatic control and the autonomy of space systems.

Mobility opportunity: like him at Polytechnique Montréal, a semester or a final year in Canada.

Role model 2 · Aerospace & defence

Guillaume Faury

Flight test engineer on the Tiger helicopter for the DGA (French defence procurement agency), then R&D director at Peugeot, CEO of Airbus Helicopters and, since 2019, CEO of Airbus.

What I take away: a career built close to products and technology, from flight testing to leading a major group.

Mobility opportunity: an internship in a European aerospace group, for example in Germany.

Role model 3 · ENSEEIHT alumnus

Laurent Rossi

An ENSEEIHT graduate, he started at Renault, moved to the United States (MBA at Harvard, consultant in New York), then led Alpine and its Formula 1 team.

What I take away: proof that an ENSEEIHT degree opens very diverse doors, from engineering to running a company.

Mobility opportunity: a double degree abroad, for example with Georgia Tech in the United States.

Experience abroad

A month of research in China

17 July – 12 August 2026 · Beihang University, Hangzhou

Research internship at the BCC Lab · École Centrale de Pékin

Research internship in simulation for medical physics, in a French-Chinese laboratory based on Beihang's international campus in Hangzhou. My first experience of living and working abroad.

What I discovered

  • A very different culture and way of life, day to day on a campus on the outskirts of Hangzhou.
  • The city of Hangzhou, and two weekends in Shanghai to discover one of the country's largest cities.
  • An easier integration thanks to my roommate, who helped me a lot to find my bearings.

How I adapted

  • Working in English with a Chinese supervisor, sometimes with the help of translation tools: I learned to phrase my questions as simply and precisely as possible.
  • Remote supervision over the summer: I had to make progress on my own, search the documentation by myself and report on my work regularly.

Beyond the scientific topic, this internship above all taught me to adapt to an unfamiliar environment and to work independently within an international team. It also made me want to learn Chinese, which I have started at ENSEEIHT.

Student associations

Founding the Centrale Méditerranée Bachelor's student union

2023 – 2026 · Centrale Méditerranée · Nice campus

Vice-president and co-founder of the Bachelor's student union (BDE)

In my very first year, I helped create the Bachelor's student union (Bureau des élèves), which did not yet exist on the Nice campus, together with two classmates: the president (Mathis Michelis), the treasurer (Maëlle Sirvent) and myself as vice-president.

From creation to handover

  • 1st year: setting up the association administratively, with a team of three.
  • 2nd year: first outings and parties organised.
  • 3rd year: with three year groups together, a richer programme: parties, events and a day of skiing, with up to twenty participants per event.
  • Handover to a new team, which took over the student union.

On a campus of about fifty students, the main challenge was to get people involved and keep the association alive. This experience taught me to carry a project over time, from its creation to its handover, and to persevere when engagement around me is limited.
